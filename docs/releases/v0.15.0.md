Runner v0.15.0: a dialect's `research` source is prepared from the research record its caller retains, bound at import to an AI profile and the web profiles its capabilities need, with twenty-six recorded vectors; a named Dialect publication keeps its artifact identity and canonical bytes from preview through execution and offline verification.

## What an operator must do

- **Nothing for existing runs.** Exports, the journal, `verify-run`'s report, its exit codes and refusal flags are byte-identical to v0.14.0's for every existing run; the twelve published vectors recompute byte for byte, and the twenty-six new ones beside them.
- **To prepare a research source**, give the installation's AI profile a `research` declaration (`capabilities`, and `model` exactly when `answer` is among them), bind the source at import or give the bound dialect, and retain the research record with every response the record names; Runner replays the record and asks no model. A profile the source is not bound to, lacking the capability or naming another model, is refused by name as `profile-mismatch`.
- **To publish a named Dialect** (`formatVersion`, `id`, `version`), the publication is admitted once per identity and version; the same pair with different bytes is refused, and a changed publication needs a newer version on a reviewed upgrade.

## Research sources prepared from retained responses (#97, closing #88)

- An input profile may declare `research: {capabilities, model}`; a research source is bound to that profile and to one web profile per web capability it asks for (`webProfiles`), at import or directly; the bound dialect adds those members and keeps everything else as written; a release freezes every assigned profile.
- Replay: the record's maxima first (`input-over-bound`), then its form (`input-malformed`), then each retained response under its own profile over its own request, with both receipt instants inside the window, the adapter compared as a whole object and the result and request digests held; the read is a copy or rule over the record's closed pointer list, or a reconciliation; targets are `generated`; `cites` follow the order of sources and each target's lineage carries `receipts`. Background acquisition refuses a research source; `/v1/inputs/next` names it with its recipe for the caller to run.
- Twenty-six vectors over the two examples of the dialect companion's plan, recorded through the Runtime and a loopback stand-in gateway, every identifier synthetic; the two whose inputs were changed after the run hold the recorded preparation fixed, marked `retained: "recorded"` in the manifest.

## Named Dialect publications pinned through job execution (#96, closing #95)

- A named Dialect (`formatVersion` `2.0.0` or `3.0.0`, `id`, `version`, per the specification's RFC 0021) preserves its identity and canonical bytes through preview, binding, release admission, execution, restart and offline verification; a release retains the original publication apart from the bound execution derivative.
- Reusing an admitted identity and version with different bytes is refused; historical integer formats are unchanged, and no new source kind is enabled by identity.
